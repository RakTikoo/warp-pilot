# GATE-25 — Literature / novelty grading: classical Dirac stress-energy claims

Date of search: 2026-11-24. Method: web search + full-text fetch only (no live browser).
Gate posture: NO novelty by default.

## Verdict summary

- **Claim A (flat-space, positive-frequency plane-wave superposition, T(k,k)<0 with ρ>0, single modes ≥0, interference-driven): NOT FOUND in the literature in this form.** The *general* fact "classical Dirac violates NEC/WEC" is textbook/known since long ago, and pointwise + averaged NEC violation by classical positive-frequency Dirac fields is now published — but in *curved wormhole backgrounds*, by a *different mechanism* (single stationary modes already violate; superpositions used only for normalizability). The specific flat-space refinement — ρ stays positive, single plane waves obey NEC, negativity is purely a multi-mode interference effect of the Hadamard-mixed Gram/frequency form — was not found, and does not follow trivially from the standard "T00 unbounded below" argument (which needs negative-frequency/Dirac-sea admixture and gives ρ<0). Residual novelty, if any, is in the *mechanism + the ρ>0 / single-mode-≥0 dichotomy in flat space*, not in "Dirac violates NEC".
- **Claim B (classical Dirac superposition with Hawking–Ellis Type IV, complex mixed-eigenvalue pair): NOT FOUND.** Authoritative reviews state flatly that *no classical matter field giving Type IV is known* (as of 2018–2021); Type IV was known only for renormalized quantum vacuum expectation values (Unruh state, scalar). No source attributes Type IV to a classical (c-number) Dirac field, single or superposed. This is the stronger of the two novelty candidates — conditional on the internal computation being correct and on the field being a genuine on-shell solution (not freely prescribed ψ, ∂ψ data at a point, which would make Type IV at a point unsurprising).

## Claim A — closest prior art

### A1. Weinbaum, "Obstructions to traversable wormholes in Einstein-Dirac theory"
Robert J. Weinbaum (Univ. of Chicago). APS April Meeting talks 2023 & 2024 ("The Average Null Energy Condition for Classical Dirac Fields"); full paper in *Phys. Rev. D* (APS listing, DOI 10.1103/g89c-gqyf; arXiv version dated 2026 in the mirror consulted — treat the arXiv no./date as unverified, the PRD version is the citable one).

What it establishes (§III C, "Dirac Fields Can Violate the Pointwise and Averaged Null Energy Conditions"):
- On fixed static, spherically symmetric *wormhole* backgrounds, a classical Dirac field "can easily violate the NEC at a point" by explicit example: because the Dirac equation is first order, the spinor components and their radial derivatives at a point are freely specifiable, and values are exhibited making the radial null contraction (his eq. 66) negative.
- A *single stationary mode* Ψ_ω0 with A(ω0,ω0) < 0 (his polarized stress-tensor kernel A(ω,ω′), eq. 73) already violates pointwise NEC. Normalizable violators are then built as *wavepackets* Ψ[a] = ∫ a(ω) Ψ_ω dω over positive frequencies supported near ω0; by continuity of A, the packet inherits the (already negative) diagonal value. Superposition is a normalizability device, **not** the source of the negativity.
- ANEC violation is then obtained by a conformal-rescaling argument on the wormhole background (massless case exact; massive by high-frequency approximation).

Assumptions: c-number classical spinor = expectation value for a *single-particle* quantum state; positive frequency w.r.t. Killing time; curved background; general (non-plane-wave) solutions; no claim that ρ>0 at the violating point, and no single-mode NEC-positivity result — indeed his single modes violate.

Relation to Claim A: This kills any novelty for "positive-frequency classical Dirac can violate pointwise NEC/ANEC" *in general*. It does **not** contain Claim A as stated: different setting (flat vs curved), different mechanism (diagonal negativity + continuity vs off-diagonal interference with non-negative diagonal), and Claim A's ρ>0 retention and single-plane-wave T(k,k)≥0 are neither stated nor implied. Note the tension the gate must resolve: Weinbaum's "single modes violate" vs the programme's "single modes always ≥0" — both can hold because "mode" means different objects (curved-space stationary spinor with free derivative data vs on-shell flat plane wave, for which T(k,k) ∝ (k·p)(k·j) with j causal). The programme should cite Weinbaum and state this distinction explicitly, or the claim reads as already-refuted.

### A2. Tóth, "Weak cosmic censorship, dyonic Kerr–Newman black holes and Dirac fields"
Gábor Zsolt Tóth, arXiv:1509.02878 [gr-qc] (2015); extending Düztaş & Semiz's Kerr/massless result (his ref. [15]).

What it establishes: for a classical Dirac test field on extremal dyonic Kerr–Newman, black-hole-destroying modes exist in a frequency band (ω, n) satisfying his ineq. (4.12), and "the possibility of the conversion is a direct consequence of the fact that the Einstein–Hilbert energy-momentum tensor of the classical Dirac field does not satisfy the null energy condition." He stresses the Dirac field "is well known not to satisfy the weak energy condition [17], in contrast with the scalar and electromagnetic fields," and carefully distinguishes the Einstein–Hilbert T_μν from Noether currents (for a mode e^{−iωt}, the Noether energy current is ω j^μ with j^μ = ψ̄γ^μψ causal — the NEC failure lives in the Hilbert tensor, at the horizon, in curved space).

Assumptions: single c-number spinor, test field, curved rotating background, horizon fluxes. No flat-space plane waves, no ρ>0 statement, no interference mechanism. This is the standard citation that classical-Dirac NEC/WEC failure is *folklore-level known* — the gate should treat the bare phenomenon as un-novel, full stop.

### A3. Kontou & Sanders, "Energy conditions in general relativity and quantum field theory"
E.-A. Kontou & K. Sanders, *Class. Quantum Grav.* 37 (2020) 193001; arXiv:2003.01815.

What it establishes (Dirac discussion, §3.2 in arXiv v2): with the Belinfante–Rosenfeld tensor T_μν = (i/2)[ψ̄γ_(μ ∇_ν)ψ − (∇_(μψ̄)γ_ν)ψ], the classical energy density T00 = (i/2)[ψ†ψ̇ − ψ̇†ψ] "is unbounded both from above and below and thus it is often stated in the literature that the classical Dirac field violates the WEC. The reason is that viewing the Dirac equation as a single-particle equation can lead to arbitrary negative energies in the 'Dirac sea'." They then pivot to QFT, where normal ordering gives a positive Hamiltonian, and review QWEIs for the *quantum* Dirac field: Vollick (2D), Fewster & Verch (Hadamard states, curved spacetime), Fewster & Mistry (explicit bound, Minkowski, gr-qc/0307098), Dawson & Fewster (curved, gr-qc/0604106), Smith (absolute QWEI).

Relevance: (i) The textbook WEC failure uses the *unrestricted* classical field (negative frequencies admitted) and produces negative *energy density* — the opposite regime from Claim A (positive-frequency only, ρ>0, only the null contraction negative). Claim A therefore does not follow trivially from this; if anything the review's framing ("quite different for the Dirac field" = a Dirac-sea artefact cured by QFT) is what Claim A's positive-frequency restriction is designed to evade, and the programme must argue against the obvious referee objection that positive-frequency restriction should restore positivity — for ρ it does (T00 = Σ ω_i |…|²-type positivity), for T(k,k) it apparently does not, because the null contraction samples the *transition* currents with the indefinite weight ½(f_i+f_j). That argument was not found pre-made in the literature. (ii) Fewster et al. is entirely quantum-expectation-value work; it bears on Claim A only as the QEI backdrop, not as prior art for the classical statement.

### A4. Vollick; Yu & Shu — quantum two-state superpositions (near-miss, different object)
- D. N. Vollick, "Negative Energy Density States for the Dirac Field in Flat Spacetime," arXiv:gr-qc/9806064 (1998), *Phys. Rev. D*: for *quantum* states that are superpositions of two single-particle electron states, ⟨:T00:⟩ is unbounded below; negative density appears as a light-speed-propagating wave on a positive background; scalar-field single-particle superpositions cannot do this.
- H. Yu & P.-X. Shu (cited as Yu), "Quantum states with negative energy density in the Dirac field and quantum inequalities," *Phys. Lett. B* 570 (2003) 123, arXiv:hep-th/0307102: superpositions of two multi-particle states give negative ⟨T00⟩ under stated particle-content conditions.

Relevance: these are the closest "superposition ⇒ negativity" results in flat space, and a referee will cite them. But they are (a) quantum expectation values of the normal-ordered operator, not the classical c-number tensor; (b) about ρ<0, not T(k,k)<0 with ρ>0; (c) two-state quantum superpositions (including vacuum/particle-number-changing sectors in Yu–Shu), not a classical multi-plane-wave field. They establish that Dirac interference can produce negative *densities* quantum-mechanically; they do not state or imply Claim A's classical null-contraction result. (Caveat: for a single-particle wavefunction the classical T_ab[ψ] and the one-particle expectation value are closely related, so the gate may judge the *distance* between Vollick and Claim A to be smaller than the programme hopes — the clean separation is ρ vs T(k,k) and the single-mode positivity, not classical-vs-quantum per se.)

### A5. Background Einstein–Dirac literature (checked, not prior art for A as stated)
- Finster & Hainzl, "A spatially homogeneous and isotropic Einstein-Dirac cosmology," arXiv:1101.1872: §4.3 analyses energy conditions, but for symmetry-reduced cosmological spinor ansätze (and bouncing solutions driven by quantum oscillations) — no plane-wave interference statement.
- Blázquez-Salcedo, Knoll & Radu, *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 126, 101102 (2021) and Konoplya & Zhidenko, *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 128, 091104 (2022): traversable Einstein-Dirac-Maxwell wormholes — they *presuppose* Dirac NEC violation at the throat; Weinbaum's critique is that they mishandle the positive-frequency/single-particle conditions. No mechanism analysis of the Claim-A type.
- Fabbri, "A geometrical assessment of spinorial energy conditions," arXiv:1702.04687: argues the energy problem for spinors is ill-posed without torsion/full gravitational coupling (Einstein–Cartan framing) — a framing objection a referee could raise, not prior art.

### Claim A verdict
- Already in literature? **The phenomenon class, yes; the claim as stated, no.**
- Trivial corollary of textbook material? **No** — the textbook route (Kontou–Sanders) needs negative frequencies and yields ρ<0; Weinbaum's route needs curved-space free derivative data and yields single-mode negativity. Neither yields "flat, positive-frequency, ρ>0, single-mode-clean, interference-only T(k,k)<0."
- Grade: **NO novelty for the headline; POSSIBLE narrow novelty for the precise flat-space interference mechanism and the Gram/Hadamard structure** — publishable only if framed exactly that narrowly, with Weinbaum (PRD), Tóth (1509.02878) and Vollick (gr-qc/9806064) cited as prior art and the mode/setting distinctions made explicit.

## Claim B — closest prior art

### B1. Martín-Moruno & Visser, "Hawking–Ellis classification of stress-energy: test-fields versus back-reaction"
P. Martín-Moruno & M. Visser, arXiv:2102.13551 (2021) (also *Class. Quantum Grav.* version).

What it establishes: full test-field vs back-reaction survey of Hawking–Ellis types. §2.2.4 (Type IV), verbatim: **"There are no known classical matter fields that lead to a type IV stress-energy tensor."** Type IV (Γ = (ρ+p_∥)² − 4f² < 0, complex eigenvalue pair, no causal eigenvector) is exhibited only for *renormalized quantum vacuum* expectation values: Roman (1986) argued the evaporating-black-hole ⟨T_ab⟩ must be Type IV near the apparent horizon; Martín-Moruno & Visser showed the Unruh vacuum is Type IV far from the horizon; Abdolrahimi, Page & Tzounis showed the massless conformal scalar in the Unruh state is Type IV everywhere outside the horizon. The paper also proves Type I is *forced* under back-reaction in many symmetric settings (static DOC, horizons, axis, single-mode Bianchi) — none of which covers a general flat-space test-field superposition, so those theorems do not obstruct Claim B, but they do mean a Type IV Dirac T_ab cannot source e.g. a static self-consistent solution.

### B2. Maeda; Martín-Moruno & Visser, "Essential core of the Hawking–Ellis types"
- H. Maeda et al., "Nonstaticity with type II, III, or IV matter field in f(R_μνρσ, g^μν) gravity," arXiv:2001.11335: repeats the consensus line — "any classical type-IV matter field is not known. However, renormalized expectation values of the energy-momentum tensor are often of type IV" — and proves static solutions are absent unless total T is Type I.
- Martín-Moruno & Visser, "Essential core of the Hawking–Ellis types," *Class. Quantum Grav.* 35 (2018), arXiv:1802.00865: Type IV_0 core "is known to arise semi-classically (in renormalized expectation values)"; Types I and IV are perturbatively stable (so a genuine classical Type IV would not be a numerical artefact class).

### B3. What the "Dirac is Type I" textbook impression actually rests on
No source found proves "the Dirac T_ab is Type I" as a theorem for a general spinor. The impression comes from restricted settings: (i) symmetry-reduced Einstein–Dirac solutions (spherical/static/cosmological ansätze) where T_ab is diagonal by symmetry — hence Type I trivially, cf. B1's back-reaction theorems; (ii) single-spinor bilinear/Fierz treatments, where for one spinor the vector current j^a = ψ̄γ^aψ is causal (future timelike or null — stated e.g. in Tóth §2.1) and the on-shell T_ab can be recast in a fluid-like form tied to j^a, which pushes intuition toward Type I. Neither covers a *superposition*: the total spinor is still a single spinor ψ = Σψ_i, but its *derivative* bilinears contain the cross terms ψ̄_i γ_(a ∂_b) ψ_j (i≠j) — equivalently complex transition currents ū_iγu_j — which are not the current of any single mode and are not constrained to be causal. (Caution for the gate: since ψ = Σψ_i is formally "a single spinor," a referee may reframe Claim B as "a single general spinor can be Type IV" and ask why Fierz-based intuition ever suggested otherwise; the honest answer is that the Type-I intuition was only ever justified in symmetric/single-mode settings, and the literature simply never classified the general classical Dirac T_ab. That is a gap in the literature, not a contradiction of a theorem.)
- Not to be confused: Radford & Klotz, *J. Phys. A* 12 (1979) 1205, classify *Dirac fields themselves* into "type I/II" by coincidence of null directions (Newman–Penrose spinor classification) — unrelated to the Hawking–Ellis type of T_ab. Do not cite it either way.

### Claim B verdict
- Already in literature? **No — and the literature affirmatively asserts the contrary state of knowledge** ("no known classical Type IV," 2018–2021 reviews).
- Trivial corollary? **No**, with one discipline condition: the example must be a genuine on-shell classical solution (plane-wave superposition in flat space qualifies on its face). If instead ψ and ∂ψ were freely prescribed at a point (Weinbaum-style data), Type IV at a point would be a weak, near-trivial observation; the claim's weight rests entirely on the on-shell, positive-frequency, finite-superposition construction.
- Grade: **NOVELTY NOT REFUTED — strongest candidate of the gate**, conditional on (1) independent verification of the eigenvalue computation, (2) explicit statement that T_ab is the standard Belinfante/Hilbert tensor of the total spinor, (3) a check that no post-2021 source has since exhibited a classical Type IV example (search window below).
