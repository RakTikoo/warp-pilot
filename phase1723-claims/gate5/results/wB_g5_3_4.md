## G5-3 — Thin-shell σ-identity (P23-1)

### Method (independent second path)

1. **Lanczos derivation from the definition.** In my code the extrinsic
   curvature is computed as K_ij = ½ £_n h_ij in the proper-distance
   coordinate l through the shell (dl = dr/√(1−b/r)), for a *generic*
   Morris–Thorne exterior (unspecified functions Φ(r), b(r), SymPy).
   Side + has dr/dl = +s, side − has dr/dl = −s, s ≡ √(1−b(a)/a), so
   every one-sided K flips sign and the jump is twice the one-sided
   value. Lanczos S^i_j = −(1/8π)([K^i_j] − δ^i_j[K]) then returns the
   surface density and pressure symbolically.
2. **Stability by numerical integration, not by the phase's closed
   form.** For the Schwarzschild family I integrated the shell
   conservation law σ′(a) = −2(σ+𝒫)/a numerically (solve_ivp,
   rtol 1e-10) with the linearised EOS 𝒫 = 𝒫₀ + β₀²(σ−σ₀), built
   V(a) = 1 − 2M/a − (m_s(a)/2a)², m_s = 4πa²σ, from the junction
   condition, and took V″(a₀) by finite differences. V″(a₀) was
   verified affine in β₀² (deviation at β₀²=0.5 ≤ 2×10⁻⁸), so
   β₀²_crit(x) follows from the two endpoint evaluations; the minimum
   on (1, 3/2) was taken on a 24-point grid.
3. **EC scan on my own coarser grid** (401 × 241 = 96,641 points,
   q ∈ [0, 0.999], A ∈ [−2, 10], a = 1 m).
4. **Tides** from the Schwarzschild orthonormal Riemann components
   (radial gradient c²·2M/a³; transverse boost factor γ²(1−v²) = 1
   checked numerically at v = 0.1c and 0.999c).
