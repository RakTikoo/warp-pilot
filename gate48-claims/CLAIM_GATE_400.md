## Leg 1 — exact-category comparison, broad → narrow

### Category A: static, spherically symmetric configurations in GR whose
stress-energy satisfies the pointwise energy conditions
**Verdict: NOT NOVEL — the category is classical and densely populated.**

Ordinary static stellar models satisfy NEC, WEC, DEC and SEC
simultaneously, globally, as a matter of routine: the Schwarzschild
constant-density interior solution (1916) and the Tolman (1939) / TOV
family are the textbook marks, and the modern stellar-model literature
verifies all four conditions as a standard acceptability check (e.g. the
relativistic neutron-star model in *Research in Astronomy and
Astrophysics* 18 (2018) 25 states on its face that "the solution
presented in this article satisfies all the energy conditions" — NEC,
WEC, SEC and DEC). Phase 400's object satisfies *fewer* conditions than
an ordinary star (its SEC is negative) over *less* scope (one radial
band, not a global solution). No first of any kind lives here.

### Category B: static configurations satisfying NEC + WEC + DEC with SEC
violated
**Verdict: NOT NOVEL — prior mark 2001.**

The Mazur–Mottola gravastar (P. O. Mazur & E. Mottola, gr-qc/0109035;
PNAS 101 (2004) 9545) is a static, spherically symmetric configuration
whose de Sitter interior (p = −ρ < 0 pressure, ρ > 0) saturates NEC
(ρ + p = 0), WEC and DEC (ρ = |p|) while violating SEC
(ρ + p_r + 2p_t = −2ρ < 0); its stiff-matter shell (p = ρ) satisfies all
four. The EC bookkeeping of this exact signature — NEC/WEC/DEC held,
SEC violated, in a static configuration — is worked over in the primary
literature, e.g. Horvat & Ilijić, "Gravastar energy conditions
revisited" (arXiv:0707.1636), which derives sharp DEC bounds for
gravastar shells, and Cattoen, Faber & Visser, "Gravastars must have
anisotropic pressures" (gr-qc/0505137), which keeps ρ positive
throughout while permitting negative interior pressure. Dark-energy-star
models populate the same signature. Phase 400's EC signature is
therefore not new even in its specific NEC/WEC/DEC-yes, SEC-no form.

### Category C: static wormhole throats without NEC violation
**Verdict: CATEGORY MISMATCH — the object is not in this category, in
either direction.**

Morris & Thorne (Am. J. Phys. 56 (1988) 395) proved that a static,
spherically symmetric traversable wormhole's flare-out condition at the
throat forces the radial tension to exceed the energy density there
(τ₀ > ρ₀c²), i.e. NEC is violated at the throat; Teo's rotating
generalisation (gr-qc/9803098) restates in its abstract that "such a
wormhole generically violates the null energy condition at the throat."
Phase 400's object has **no throat**: no shape function with
b(r₀) = r₀, no flare-out, no second asymptotic region, no redshift/
shape-function structure of the Morris–Thorne form at all. It therefore
neither violates nor evades the Morris–Thorne result, and any wording
of the form "first static configuration with no NEC violation at a
throat" would be a category error — there is no throat for the
condition to hold at. This category confers nothing and is not beaten.

### Category D: the warp-drive literature's zero-shift / zero-velocity
limit
**Verdict: NOT NOVEL — the limit is degenerate in the literature, and
the no-go results do not cover (or need) a static object.**

In Alcubierre's 1994 metric the shift enters as v_s·f(r_s); at
v_s = 0 the line element is exactly Minkowski space, which satisfies
every energy condition trivially with T = 0 (the line element is
reproduced in Bobrick & Martire, Class. Quantum Grav. 38 (2021) 105009,
§1). Bobrick & Martire's general framework defines a warp drive as a
shell of material *moving* at velocity v_s; their positive-energy
constructions are subluminal **moving** shells, and at v_s = 0 their
object degenerates to a static shell — a star-like configuration of
Category A, not a drive. Santiago, Schuster & Visser (Phys. Rev. D 105
(2022) 064038; arXiv:2105.03079) prove that all physically reasonable
Natário-class warp drives — a class defined by a nonzero bubble flow —
violate NEC (and hence WEC, SEC, DEC). A strictly static S ≡ 0
configuration is outside that class by construction: Phase 400's object
neither breaks their theorem nor is touched by it, which is the same
fact as Phase 400's own fence — it has no drive content. Lentz's
positive-energy solitons (Class. Quantum Grav. 38 (2021) 075015) are
**moving** (nonzero shift), claim Eulerian WEC only, and admit DEC as
an open challenge in the author's own summary (arXiv:2201.00652); they
are not a mark in this category and this object is not a mark in
theirs. Conclusion: "first EC-nonnegative zero-shift warp configuration"
is not an available claim — the zero-shift limit was never the
contested ground, and where it is defined at all it is flat or
star-like.
