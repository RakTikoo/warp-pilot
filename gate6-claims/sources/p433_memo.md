## (c) MIXTURE-CLASS TUBE SUPPORT — component-blindness

### Claim under test
For the tube's required smeared ⟨T_μνu^μu^ν⟩ profile along a
wall worldline — total fixed by G/8π of the tube metric —
the Ford–Roman/Fewster bound applicable to the TOTAL is
independent of any decomposition T = ΣᵢTᵢ, except through
the species factor (N free fields ⇒ ≤ N× the single-field
bound, A4).

### Proof (free-field scope, the A4/QEI domain)
Use the QEI in the form this programme uses (Fewster–Eveson
/ Fewster–Teo; P27-3 A3/A4, P29-4 §1): for one free field in
a Hadamard state, along an inertial/static worldline with
sampling function f = g²,
∫⟨T(u,u)⟩ f dt ≥ −c_species · Φ[f],
where Φ[f] ≥ 0 is a functional of the sampler and the
background spectral density **only** (e.g. Φ = 𝒬[f] of
P29-4 §1), and c_species is the field's constant (massless
scalar: the 3/(32π²) class; Maxwell: 2× scalar, A4).
Now fix the field content: fields 1…N present, each in any
state, T_tot = Σᵢ Tᵢ (the total is whatever it is — for the
tube it is pinned to G/8π of the metric, which only fixes
the REQUIRED value, not the bound). Summing the per-field
inequalities — each is a state-independent lower bound, so
no property of the individual ⟨Tᵢ⟩ beyond its own QEI is
used:
∫⟨T_tot(u,u)⟩ f dt = Σᵢ ∫⟨Tᵢ(u,u)⟩ f dt ≥ −(Σᵢ cᵢ)·Φ[f].
The applicable bound for the total is therefore
**B_tot = (Σᵢ cᵢ)·Φ[f]**, a function of (field content,
sampler, background) only. It contains no reference to how
the required total is *attributed* among the components:
reallocating the required density between existing fields
changes each ⟨Tᵢ⟩'s required profile but changes neither
any cᵢ, nor Φ, nor the sum — the lower bound each component
must respect is a constant of the field, not of its share.
∎ (decomposition-invariance for fixed field content).
A "decomposition" can enlarge B_tot only by changing the
field content itself — adding fields — and then the
enlargement is exactly Σᵢcᵢ over the enlarged content:
for N identical scalars, N × the single-field bound (A4's
exact linearity); in general ≤ N·maxᵢcᵢ. That is the
species factor, and it is the whole story: there is no
decomposition of a fixed total, into components each
respecting its own QEI, whose applicable total bound
exceeds (Σᵢcᵢ)Φ[f]. A would-be counterexample must either
(a) hold a component below its own QEI floor (excluded by
hypothesis), (b) change Φ[f] — but Φ is fixed by the
sampler and the background geometry, which the fixed total
(= fixed metric) already pins, or (c) add fields — the
species case, priced below. This is the sampling-bound
analogue of N4's linearity, one level up: N4 says the
classical total cannot be assembled from legal parts; this
says the quantum allowance for the total cannot be
inflated by bookkeeping. It is also the exact dual of
P40-3: there a constraint sector's budget was fixed
upstream of the split; here the QEI allowance is fixed by
the field content upstream of the split.
Scope stated honestly: proven for free fields (the domain
in which QEIs of this form are theorems — A1/A4).
Interacting-component QEIs are not theorem-grade in 4D
(A1: one interacting model tested here, corrections only
tightened the bound); a mixture analogue through genuinely
interacting components is outside the proof and is the
reopening condition, not a gap in it.

### The species escape, priced

species alone requires
**N ≥ 1.06 × 10⁷²** free fields (1.06e73 for the L = a
line). Read against the record's species discussions:
- A4 (P27-3): the linear-in-N law is theorem; **any cap on
  N is heuristic, not a QEI theorem** — so this pricing is

- Phase 23 GJW follow-up (p232_memo.md): the species bound
  N ≲ K(r_h/ℓ)^{d−1} with K ≲ ℓ^{d−1}/G_N; the programme

  ℓ = a = 1 m, ℓ²/l_P² ≈ 3.8e69 falls ~2.4 orders short.
- Dvali species scale (cited in p273_audit.md): Λ ~

**Verdict (c): COMPONENT-BLIND (PROOF)** — the mixture
