## The claims as tasked

- **G15-1.** A POSED TWO-MOUTH configuration in conformal (Weyl)
  gravity: two Mannheim–Kazanas-exterior wormhole mouths (the
  Gate-9-verified single-mouth member: static spherical throat,
  b(r) = r₀(r/r₀)^0.1, joined at r_Σ = 10³r₀ to an MK exterior with
  a legal shell) joined consistently in one constructed linearised
  two-centre Bach exterior, modes ℓ ≤ 3, full Darmois/conformal-join
  condition set closing, joining shells legal (positive surface
  density) at BOTH mouths. Formation pre-existing only.
- **G15-2.** A measured shortcut (effective transit accounting)
  computed IN that constructed two-mouth metric (Phase 122 P122-2:
  exterior route scored in the constructed metric's own lapse and
  proper length; thresholds re-derived in both accountings at
  D/r_Σ ∈ {10, 30, 100}).

Terminology warning that governs the whole audit: in most of the
wormhole literature "two mouths" means the two ends of *one*
throat (two asymptotic regions). G15-1 means something different
and stronger: **two separate mouths, at a finite separation D,
embedded in one common exterior**, each mouth being the exterior
opening of the Gate-9 member. Papers using the generic usage are
near neighbours only at the level of words, and are marked as such
below. Two further naming collisions were actively excluded:
"Bach–Weyl" (the 1921 GR two-body solution) is not the Bach tensor,
and "Weyl metrics" (the GR axisymmetric vacuum class) is not Weyl
conformal gravity.

## 2. Near neighbours — Angle 2 first (same theory home, single mouth)

These establish that the single-mouth member's theory home is
populated; none of them poses a second mouth in a common exterior.

### 2.1 Lobo (2008) — the founding conformal-Weyl wormhole class
Francisco S. N. Lobo, "General class of wormhole geometries in
conformal Weyl gravity", Class. Quantum Grav. 25 (2008) 175006;
arXiv:0801.4401.
Poses: exact single-throat Morris–Thorne-class geometries in
conformal Weyl gravity; energy conditions can hold in the throat
neighbourhood, unlike GR. Distance from G15-1: same theory home;
single centre, exact, spherical; no MK-exterior join audit of the
Gate-9 type, no second mouth, no linearised two-centre exterior,
no transit accounting in a constructed exterior (so also distant
from G15-2).

### 2.2 Varieschi & Ault (2016) — energy conditions + the junction caveat
Gabriele U. Varieschi & Ka Chun Ault, "Wormhole geometries in
fourth-order conformal Weyl gravity", Int. J. Mod. Phys. D 25
(2016) 1650064; arXiv:1510.05054.
Poses: classic Morris–Thorne geometries re-read in conformal
gravity; for particular shape/redshift choices the main energy
conditions are not violated at/near the throat; the paper itself
notes exotic matter might still be needed *at the junction* to
flat spacetime, and states the generic Morris–Thorne consequence
that effective superluminal motion is a viable consequence of the
metric. Distance: same theory home; single throat; the junction
is flagged as the open point rather than closed with a legal
shell; no two-centre construction; its "effective superluminal"
line is the generic framing behind G15-2's concept, not a
computation in a constructed two-mouth metric.

### 2.3 Oliva, Tempo & Troncoso (2009) — vacuum conformal wormhole
Julio Oliva, David Tempo & Ricardo Troncoso, "Static wormholes in
vacuum for conformal gravity", Int. J. Mod. Phys. A 24 (2009)
1528; arXiv:0907.1128.
Poses: an exact *vacuum* (Bach-flat) static wormhole, one
integration constant (neck size) connecting two homogeneous
universes, obtained by matching Einstein spacetimes via improper
conformal transformations. Distance: same theory home and
vacuum sector; single neck between two universes (generic
"two mouths" usage), not two mouths in one common exterior; no
shell-legality read at two mouths; no transit accounting.

### 2.4 Hohmann, Pfeifer, Raidal & Veermäe (2018) — conformal-transformation wormholes
M. Hohmann, C. Pfeifer, M. Raidal & H. Veermäe, "Wormholes in
conformal gravity", JCAP 10 (2018) 003, DOI
10.1088/1475-7516/2018/10/003; arXiv:1802.02184.
Poses: a general construction — cut + local conformal
transformation turns any static spherical spacetime into a
wormhole; explicit traversable and non-traversable examples
locally conformal to Schwarzschild-(A)dS; exact vacuum solutions
in Weyl gravity and conformal scalar-tensor theories; charged
examples. Distance: same theory home; the method is intrinsically
single-centre/spherical (a conformal factor on one spacetime);
it does not and cannot as stated produce two mouths at a
separation D in one exterior, imposes no BDE shell-legality
condition at two mouths, and computes no exterior-route transit
accounting. This is the strongest "wormholes are generic in
conformal gravity" neighbour — it makes the *existence* of
conformal wormholes unsurprising while leaving the G15-1 pose
(a specific two-centre matching problem) untouched.

### 2.5 Cadoni, Modesto, Pitzalis & Sanna (2025) — stable conformal wormholes
Mariano Cadoni, Leonardo Modesto, Mirko Pitzalis & Andrea P.
Sanna, "Stable wormholes in conformal gravity", JCAP 06 (2025)
016, DOI 10.1088/1475-7516/2025/06/016; arXiv:2503.14214.
Poses: traversable wormholes by Weyl rescaling of Minkowski
space; the detailed example is a Morris–Thorne shape function in
a conformally invariant *scalar-tensor* theory; regular,
traversable, and stable at linear-perturbation level. Distance:
conformal-gravity family but a different sector (scalar-tensor,
conformally flat seed) from the programme's pure-Weyl/Bach,
Ω = 1, MK-exterior member; single throat; no two-centre pose;
no shell legality at two mouths. Gate-9 already recorded this
paper as holding the bare headline "stable wormholes in
conformal gravity" for its class — G15-1/G15-2 make no such
headline claim, so there is no collision, but the paper bounds
any loose wording.

### 2.6 Bochicchio & Laserra (2013) — the MK thin-shell neighbour (closest MK hit)
Ivana Bochicchio & Ettore Laserra, "Intra-Galactic thin shell
wormhole and its stability", Int. J. Theor. Phys. 52 (2013);
arXiv:1301.1532.
Poses: a thin-shell wormhole joining **two copies** of the
Mannheim–Kazanas–de Sitter spacetime in conformal gravity,
cut-and-paste at one throat shell with Chaplygin-gas shell
matter; stability under spherical perturbations (generally
unstable, a small stable radius interval). Distance — and this
distinction is the audit's most important single call: joining
two *copies* of an MK exterior back-to-back at one shell is the
standard single-throat construction. It is **not** two mouths
embedded at separation D in *one* common exterior, it has one
shell not two, it uses GR-style (Eiroa/Israel) thin-shell
stability machinery rather than a full BDE conformal join set
closed mode-by-mode at ℓ ≤ 3, and it is exact/spherical rather
than a linearised two-centre (necessarily non-spherical) Bach
problem. Same exterior family (MK), different pose.

### 2.7 Background MK references (not wormhole poses)
Mannheim & Kazanas, Astrophys. J. 342 (1989) 635 (the MK exterior
itself); Bhattacharya et al., arXiv:0910.1112 (MK solution
features); Berezin, Dokuchaev & Eroshenko, arXiv:1412.2917
(spherical conformal gravity; MK as the varying-conformal-scalar
class). None poses a wormhole join of the G15 type.

## 3. Near neighbours — Angle 4 (junction conditions in conformal gravity)

### 3.1 Berezin, Dokuchaev & Eroshenko (2018) — the BDE formalism itself
Victor Berezin, Vyacheslav Dokuchaev & Yury Eroshenko, "On the
theory of spherically symmetric thin shells in conformal
gravity", arXiv:1710.10438 (published record: Phys. Rev. D 97
(2018) 084011, as carried in Gate-9).
What it does: constructs the thin-shell theory for (1) GR,
(2) pure conformal (Weyl) gravity, (3) Weyl+Einstein gravity,
for **spherically symmetric** shells; derives the matching
conditions in each theory and compares with Israel's; finds
peculiar features of the pure-Weyl case, notably that for
traceless shells the dynamics cannot be derived from the
matching conditions (the shell trajectory is arbitrary), while
in Weyl+Einstein it is fully restored. This is the published
literature home of the programme's "BDE pattern".
Distance from G15-1: the formalism is known — G15-1 does not
claim it. What BDE do *not* do: apply it (or any extension of it)
to a non-spherical, two-centre, linearised multipole matching —
modes ℓ = 1…3, an angular Darmois pair, and a shell-sign read on
a solved two-mouth configuration at both mouths. No such
application was found anywhere in this search. The programme's
use is an *application/extension* of a published formalism to a
pose the formalism's authors did not treat; that is exactly the
PARTIALLY KNOWN structure of the grade.

### 3.2 Fourth-order / f(R) junction literature (different theory homes)
Deruelle, Sasaki & Sendouda, "Surface terms and matching
conditions in f(R) gravity" (INSPIRE full text read); the
generalised Darmois–Israel literature for quadratic gravity
(MDPI review read in excerpt, which itself notes earlier
quadratic-gravity junction proposals were incomplete); Senovilla
and others on double layers in higher-order gravity (via Gate-9's
log). What they establish: junction conditions in fourth-order
theories are theory-specific, are richer than Israel's (extra
continuity conditions, possible double layers), and must be
derived per theory. Distance: none of these is conformal (Weyl)
gravity, none treats a wormhole two-mouth pose, and none
supplies the specific conformal join scalars ([R̃], [R̃_,n]) the
programme uses — those come from the BDE spherical analysis
(§3.1) as generalised by the programme. They bound G15-1 only
by showing the *genre* (fourth-order junction conditions) is
established literature.

## 4. Near neighbours — Angles 3 and 5 (GR / other homes: the pose and the shortcut exist there)

These are the neighbours that prevent any NOVEL grade. They are
in **different theory homes** and are named as such; they must
not be conflated with G15-1/G15-2, and G15-1/G15-2 must not be
described in ways that erase the theory-home difference in
either direction.

### 4.1 Schein, Aichelburg & Israel (1996) — the closest pose analogue to G15-1
F. Schein, P. C. Aichelburg & W. Israel, "String Supported
Wormhole Spacetimes and Causality Violations",
arXiv:gr-qc/9602053 (1996). Primary source read (Q23).
What it poses: in **GR**, a static axisymmetric wormhole built
from the field of two Schwarzschild particles held in equilibrium
by strings to infinity; the wormhole is obtained by matching two
timelike surfaces, one surrounding each particle, so the two
mouths sit in one common asymptotically flat exterior;
the matching is **not exact in general**, with error made
arbitrarily small when mouth separation ≫ mouth radius; unequal
mouth masses yield causality-violating effects.
Distance from G15-1: this *is* the two-mouths-in-a-common-exterior
pose, in GR, 30 years earlier — so the pose concept is known.
Every technical element of G15-1 differs: theory (Einstein vs
Bach/conformal), mouths (Schwarzschild-particle surfaces vs
MK-exterior members with Gate-9 interiors), matching (approximate,
error suppressed by separation, with strings/struts holding the
mouths apart vs a linearised Bach solve closing the full
condition set mode-by-mode at ℓ ≤ 3 with no strut), and shell
legality (no positive-surface-density-at-both-mouths theorem of
the G15 type). It is the single closest near neighbour to G15-1
and the reason "two-mouth pose" alone cannot be claimed as new
in any wording.

### 4.2 Maldacena, Milekhin & Popov; Fu, Grado-White & Marolf; Emparan et al. — GR/quantum two-mouth constructions + transit times
- Juan Maldacena, Alexey Milekhin & Fedor Popov, "Traversable
  wormholes in four dimensions", arXiv:1807.04726; Class.
  Quantum Grav. 40 (2023) 155016. Two oppositely magnetically
  charged near-extremal black-hole mouths joined by a long
  AdS₂×S² throat, held open by fermion Casimir energy;
  Einstein–Maxwell + quantum fermions.
- Juan Maldacena & Alexey Milekhin, "Humanly traversable
  wormholes", Phys. Rev. D 103 (2021) 066007, DOI
  10.1103/PhysRevD.103.066007. Randall–Sundrum variant; states
  explicitly that the through-trip takes longer (for the outside
  observer) than travelling between the mouths outside — i.e.
  in this accounting there is **no shortcut**, by construction
  (causality). A useful contrast neighbour for G15-2: a
  published two-mouth accounting whose answer is the opposite
  sign, in a different theory home.
- Zicao Fu, Brianna Grado-White & Donald Marolf, "Traversable
  Asymptotically Flat Wormholes with Short Transit Times",
  arXiv:1908.03273; Class. Quantum Grav. 36 (2019) 245018.
  The closest Angle-5 neighbour: transit time is the paper's
  title quantity; for mouth separation d, minimum transit time
  t_min = d + logs, computed in a GR construction with quantum
  backreaction (cosmic strings holding the mouths apart).
  Distance from G15-2: the transit time is computed for a
  *through*-trip in a specific GR+quantum geometry whose
  exterior is ambient asymptotically flat space; it is not an
  exterior-route-vs-through-route accounting evaluated inside a
  constructed two-centre metric with the exterior's own lapse
  and proper length, and the theory home is not conformal
  gravity.
- Roberto Emparan, Brianna Grado-White, Donald Marolf & Marija
  Tomašević, "Multi-mouth Traversable Wormholes",
  arXiv:2012.07821; JHEP 05 (2021) 032. Extends the two-mouth
  GR+quantum construction to ≥ 3 mouths traversable between any
  pair. Distance: same as MMP — GR+quantum, no Bach system, no
  conformal join set, no constructed-exterior shortcut accounting
  of the G15-2 type.

### 4.3 Other GR multi-throat / multi-wormhole constructions (weaker neighbours)
- Gary W. Gibbons & Mikhail S. Volkov, "Weyl metrics and
  wormholes", JCAP 05 (2017) 039, arXiv:1701.05533; and "Ring
  wormholes via duality rotations", Phys. Lett. B 760 (2016)
  324–328, arXiv:1606.04879. GR axisymmetric (Weyl-class)
  vacuum/scalar multi-wormholes from one- and two-rod solutions,
  with rings/struts. Exact, GR; multi-*asymptotic-region*
  topology rather than two MK mouths in one exterior.
- A. I. Egorov, P. E. Kashargin & Sergey V. Sushkov, "Scalar
  multi-wormholes", Class. Quantum Grav. 33 (2016) 175011,
  arXiv:1603.09552. GR + phantom scalar; N-wormhole superposition
  by the Bach–Weyl method (GR naming collision, §0); 2^N
  asymptotic regions. Exact, single-field home, no common-exterior
  two-mouth matching at finite D of the G15 type.
- Mauricio Cataldo, Antonella Cid & Pedro Labraña, "Two-throat
  asymptotically flat wormholes", Eur. Phys. J. C 86 (2026) 402,
  DOI 10.1140/epjc/s10052-026-15704-1. GR; two throats *in series*
  along one Morris–Thorne tunnel (an intermediate cavity), with
  throat separation controlling exoticity. Not two mouths in a
  common exterior — a radial-chain neighbour only. (Note the
  2026 journal date as returned by the publisher page; the audit
  records it as returned.)
- Lobo et al., "Exact ultrastatic multi-throat wormholes",
  arXiv:2611.24965 (as returned). GR hierarchy of N+1 throats and
  N anti-throats in one radial chain; exact. Same radial-chain
  distance as Cataldo et al.

### 4.4 The generic shortcut framing (background for G15-2)
Morris & Thorne (1988) define the wormhole shortcut; Visser,
*Lorentzian Wormholes* (1995), and the Lobo review (arXiv:
0710.4474) state the standard form: wormholes/warp geometries
allow "effective" superluminal travel while local light speed is
never surpassed. Varieschi & Ault (§2.2) repeat this framing
inside conformal gravity for a single throat. So: the *concept*
measured by G15-2 — effective transit accounting, local causality
intact — is textbook. What was not found is its *evaluation
inside a constructed two-mouth metric*: in the published
two-mouth work the exterior is ambient (flat/AdS/RS) and the
interesting number is the through-time; in G15-2 (per Phase 122)
the exterior leg itself is recomputed in the linearised
two-centre Bach metric (L_ext/D = 0.775 / 0.755 / 0.463 and the
lapse correction at the registered rows), moving both break-even
thresholds. That specific accounting step is the part not found.

- **Full texts vs abstracts:** full text (or full-text excerpts

## 6. Bottom line for the gate

- **G15-1: PARTIALLY KNOWN.** Not found: any two-mouth /
  two-centre wormhole pose in conformal (Weyl) gravity, any
  application of conformal junction conditions to a two-centre
  multipole matching, any linearised Bach two-centre exterior
  with shells legal at both mouths. Known, and to be cited
  whenever the claim is stated: the single-mouth conformal
  wormhole literature (§2, esp. Lobo; Varieschi & Ault; Hohmann
  et al.; Cadoni et al.), the MK thin-shell two-copy construction
  (Bochicchio & Laserra), the BDE junction formalism itself
  (arXiv:1710.10438), and the GR two-mouth pose (Schein,
  Aichelburg & Israel, gr-qc/9602053; MMP line).
- **G15-2: PARTIALLY KNOWN.** Not found: a shortcut/transit
  accounting evaluated inside a constructed two-mouth metric
  (exterior leg in the constructed metric's own lapse and proper
  length, thresholds re-derived), in conformal gravity or any
  other theory reached. Known, and to be cited: the effective-
  shortcut concept (Morris–Thorne; Visser; Lobo review;
  Varieschi & Ault inside conformal gravity) and published
  two-mouth transit-time computations in GR+quantum homes
  (Fu, Grado-White & Marolf arXiv:1908.03273; MMP), including
  one published accounting whose verdict is *no* shortcut
  (Maldacena & Milekhin, PRD 103 (2021) 066007).
- Carried clauses unchanged by this audit: formation
  PRE-EXISTING ONLY; the pose is linearised (ℓ ≤ 3 entering
  orders; channels above ε⁴ and O(ε²) nonlinear terms unposed,
  per Phase 122); (V-b) UNDECIDED: Pais–Uhlenbeck spin-2 ghost
  vs Mannheim PT-symmetric quantisation — opposed literature

  literature found here decides (V-b), and no near neighbour
